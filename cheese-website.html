About Cheese

Cheese is a type of dairy product produced in a range of flavors, textures, and forms by curdling of milk, usually from cows, goats or sheep, and sometimes of water buffalo. During production, milk is usually acidified and either the enzymes of rennet or bacterial enzymes with similar activity are added to cause the protein casein to coagulate. The solid curds, consisting of proteins and milkfat, are then separated from the liquid whey and pressed into finished cheese.

Cheese Types

There are over a thousand types of cheese produced worldwide, including:

  • Hard cheeses: Cheddar, Parmesan, Romano
  • Soft cheeses: Brie, Camembert, Ricotta
  • Blue cheeses: Gorgonzola, Roquefort, Stilton
  • Fresh cheeses: Mozzarella, Feta, Goat cheese

Nutritional Facts

Cheese is valued for its portability, long shelf life, and high content of fat, protein, calcium, and phosphorus. A 17-gram (one slice) serving of cheddar cheese contains about 4 grams of protein and 120 milligrams of calcium. Cheese is more compact and has a longer shelf life than milk.
